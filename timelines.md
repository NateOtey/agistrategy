**Definition:** Forecasts of when AGI, transformative AI, or some other milestone will arrive, usually expressed as probabilities over a range of dates.

**Example or analogy:** Good AI forecasts resemble weather reports: "a 50% chance of transformative AI by 2035" is a claim about odds, and it should move as new evidence comes in. The spread of serious estimates is wide. The AI 2027 scenario describes superhuman AI researchers arriving within a few years; Ben Todd makes the case for AGI by around 2030; Ege Erdil of Epoch argues for multi-decade timelines; and a 2023 survey of thousands of AI researchers put the median estimate for machines outperforming humans at every task at 2047. (Predictions of human-level AI "within twenty years" have a long history. Herbert Simon made one in 1965.)

**Relevance to strategy:** Timelines determine which strategies are worth pursuing. Short timelines favor actions that pay off quickly, such as changing the policies of existing labs or using institutions already in place. Long timelines favor slower investments, such as building new research fields, training people, and negotiating treaties. Because the uncertainty is so large, Toby Ord argues for "broad timelines": choosing plans that hold up reasonably well across many possible arrival dates, instead of betting everything on one.

**Where people disagree:** Nearly everything about the forecast, including which trends to extrapolate (compute, benchmarks, revenue, task length), how much bottlenecks will slow progress, and whether current methods can reach AGI at all. Forecasters also disagree about how much weight to give to insiders at frontier labs, who have the best information and the strongest incentives.
