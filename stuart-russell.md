**Who he is:** A Berkeley computer scientist, co-author of the standard AI textbook, and founder of the Center for Human-Compatible AI. His book [*Human Compatible*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Compatible) (2019) made the case that AI built to pursue fixed objectives is dangerous by design.

**What he wants:** AI that is provably beneficial and uncertain about human preferences, and binding "red lines," such as no self-replication or breaking into other computer systems, enforced by governments.

**Resources and levers:** Academic authority, the textbook that trained a generation of AI researchers, and testimony before legislatures worldwide. He leads the International Association for Safe and Ethical AI.

**Obstacles and opponents:** Industry, which resists binding rules. Skeptics of AI risk, and those who think "provably safe" AI is impractical.
