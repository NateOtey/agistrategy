**Who they are:** A Missouri Republican and a Connecticut Democrat who lead the Senate Judiciary subcommittee that has held many of Congress's sharpest AI hearings. They often legislate together.

**What they want:** Accountability for AI companies. Their 2025 AI Risk Evaluation Act would require developers of the most advanced systems to submit them for government testing before release, and their GUARD Act would bar AI companions for minors. Hawley frames the issue as protecting workers and families from Big Tech; Blumenthal, as consumer protection and liability.

**Resources and levers:** Subpoena-backed investigations (Hawley opened one into Meta's chatbot policies in 2025), hearings with whistleblowers and grieving parents, and the unusual credibility of a bipartisan pair. Parts of their ideas appear in the Republican [TRUMP AMERICA AI Act](https://techpolicy.press/trump-and-gop-lawmakers-push-for-new-national-ai-legislation), including a Department of Energy program to evaluate advanced AI.

**Obstacles and opponents:** The tech industry and its super PACs. Senate leaders and committee chairs, including Ted Cruz, who favor lighter rules. The White House preemption push and its preference for voluntary industry commitments.
