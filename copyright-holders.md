**Who they are:** News organizations, authors, artists, music labels and studios suing AI companies for training on their work without permission or payment. The *New York Times* suit against OpenAI and Microsoft (2023) is the flagship case.

**What they want:** Compensation, licensing deals, and control over how their work is used. Some want to stop unlicensed training altogether; many are happy to settle for a market in which AI companies pay.

**Resources and levers:** Lawsuits, with very large potential damages. In 2025 Anthropic agreed to pay $1.5 billion to settle a class action by book authors. Licensing deals, like those signed by news publishers and music labels with several AI firms.

**Obstacles and opponents:** AI companies, which argue that training is fair use, and some court rulings that have agreed. The White House's 2026 framework, which leaves the question to the courts. The slow pace of litigation compared to the pace of AI.
