**What it is:** The Center for AI Standards and Innovation, part of the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST). It was founded in 2023 as the US AI Safety Institute and renamed in June 2025.

**What it wants:** To measure what frontier models can do, especially in cyber, biological and chemical threats, and to develop standards that help US companies and the government use AI securely. With the renaming, its mission shifted from "safety" toward national security and promoting American AI.

**Resources and levers:** Agreements with leading labs to test models before release, technical staff, and NIST's credibility as a standards body. Its evaluations of Chinese models such as DeepSeek have shaped Washington's view of the competition. The independent audits promised under the 2026 White House Accord could make it more central, or route around it.

**Obstacles and opponents:** Small budgets and staff compared to the labs it evaluates. Voluntary testing that companies can decline. Political pressure on what it is allowed to say. Skeptics in the administration who see safety work as a brake.
