**What it is:** The EU's executive arm, which enforces the [AI Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_Intelligence_Act) through its AI Office. The Act entered into force in 2024, and its obligations for general-purpose AI models began in August 2025.

**What it wants:** "Trustworthy AI" that protects fundamental rights, a single EU market for AI, and, increasingly, competitiveness and "tech sovereignty." In May 2026 EU lawmakers [agreed to postpone](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines/) some of the Act's requirements for high-risk systems as part of a simplification package.

**Resources and levers:** The power to fine companies up to 7% of global revenue and to set rules for a market of 450 million people. The general-purpose AI code of practice, which most major labs signed and Meta refused. The "Brussels effect," by which companies adopt EU rules worldwide.

**Obstacles and opponents:** The Trump administration, which has threatened retaliation over EU tech rules. US tech companies' lobbying. European industry and member states that want looser rules to catch up. Its own lack of frontier labs.
