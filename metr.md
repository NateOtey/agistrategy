**What it is:** [Model Evaluation and Threat Research](https://metr.org), a Berkeley nonprofit founded by Beth Barnes, formerly of OpenAI. It evaluates frontier models for dangerous autonomous capabilities, often before release.

**What it wants:** For the world to know how capable AI agents are getting and how fast, early enough to act on it. Its best-known finding is that the length of tasks AI agents can complete has been [doubling roughly every seven months](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/).

**Resources and levers:** Pre-release access to models from several leading labs, a widely cited measurement method, and a reputation for careful, sometimes deflationary results: a [2025 study](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/) found experienced developers worked about 19% slower with AI tools, despite believing they were faster. Its role is growing: Dario Amodei's 2026 pacing proposal names METR as the kind of evaluator that should have employee-like access inside the labs.

**Obstacles and opponents:** Dependence on labs' willingness to grant access and time before release. Evaluations that may miss capabilities models hide or that only show up after deployment. Small size relative to the labs it evaluates.
