**Definition:** Differential technological development, a term from the philosopher Nick Bostrom, is the strategy of speeding up protective technologies and slowing down dangerous ones, so that defenses arrive before the threats they guard against. d/acc, a term coined by Vitalik Buterin in 2023, applies the idea in a pro-technology spirit; the "d" stands for defensive, and also for decentralized, democratic, and differential.

**Example or analogy:** A city that expects more fires can respond in several ways. It can ban matches, which is unenforceable and costly. It can do nothing, which is reckless. Or it can invest heavily in smoke detectors, sprinklers, fire codes, and fire departments, so that the city becomes resilient to fire whatever happens with the matches. In AI, protective technologies might include tools that detect new pathogens early, air filtration and ultraviolet light that kill airborne viruses, AI systems that find and fix software vulnerabilities before attackers exploit them, and methods for verifying information in an age of convincing fakes.

**Relevance to strategy:** d/acc offers a middle path between accelerationism and calls to pause. It asks which technologies to accelerate, which is often more tractable, and more politically popular, than asking everyone to slow down. It also favors decentralized defenses that do not depend on trusting any single government or company, which appeals to those worried about power concentration. Buterin's response to the AI 2027 scenario, assigned in the course's objections session, applies this lens and argues that defensive technology could make catastrophic outcomes less likely than the scenario suggests.

**Where people disagree:** Whether defense can actually keep pace with offense in the domains that matter most, such as biology and cybersecurity, and whether d/acc offers a full strategy or only a valuable complement to others. Critics note that a sufficiently capable misaligned AI would face little resistance from smoke detectors of any kind.
