**Definition:** Two alternative strategies that try to avoid some of the hardest problems posed by goal-pursuing AI agents.

- **Non-agentic AI:** systems designed to predict, explain, or answer questions without pursuing goals of their own. Yoshua Bengio's proposal for a "Scientist AI," for example, is a disinterested predictor whose only job is to estimate what is true.
- **Guaranteed safe AI:** approaches that aim for mathematical guarantees of safety. They typically combine three parts: a model of the world, a precise specification of what counts as safe, and a verifier that checks whether an AI's proposed actions satisfy that specification.

**Example or analogy:** A weather forecaster tells you whether it will rain; a farmer decides what to plant. The forecaster can be extremely capable without wanting anything for itself, so it has no reason to deceive you or seek power. Non-agentic AI aims to be the forecaster. Guaranteed safe AI is closer to how engineers certify a bridge: the design is checked against physical laws and load requirements before anyone drives across it.

**Relevance to strategy:** Both approaches offer a way around the dilemma that capable agents may develop dangerous goals. A trustworthy non-agentic AI could also serve as a monitor or advisor for agentic systems, and as a tool for accelerating science without handing over control. They face a practical obstacle, though: the commercial value of AI increasingly lies in agents that act on their own, so these approaches swim against strong market currents. Supporting them may require deliberate funding or policy choices.

**Where people disagree:** Whether non-agentic systems stay non-agentic once people wrap them in software that acts on their predictions, and whether the messy real world can be modeled precisely enough for mathematical guarantees to mean much. Supporters reply that partial guarantees, applied to the highest-stakes domains, would still be a large improvement on none.
