**Definition:** Government rules that restrict the sale or transfer of goods, software, or technology to other countries or foreign persons. In AI, they have mainly targeted advanced chips and the equipment used to make them, and they have begun to reach AI models themselves.

**Example or analogy:** In October 2022, the United States restricted exports of advanced AI chips and chipmaking equipment to China, and it tightened those rules several times afterward. The logic is the same as keeping a rival army short of fuel: if compute drives AI progress, limiting a competitor's access to the best chips slows its progress. In June 2026, export control authority was applied to models directly. Citing national security, the US Department of Commerce suspended access by all foreign nationals to two of Anthropic's models, Claude Fable 5 and Claude Mythos 5, which led Anthropic to disable them for all customers. Anthropic disputed the basis for the directive; the Department lifted the controls on June 30, and access was restored on July 1.

**Relevance to strategy:** Export controls are the main tool the United States and its allies use to preserve a lead in AI, and they connect to nearly every other strategic question. Supporters argue that a lead buys time for safety work and gives democracies leverage to set global rules, and that chips can later serve as bargaining chips in an international agreement. Critics argue that controls intensify the race, push rivals to build their own chip industries, and make cooperation harder. Enforcement is a recurring challenge, since chips are small, valuable, and easy to smuggle through third countries.

**Where people disagree:** Whether the controls have worked; Chinese labs have continued to produce competitive models, which some read as evidence of failure and others as evidence of what Chinese labs could have done with more chips. Debate also continues over whether the controls make a US–China agreement more or less likely, and over how far they should extend, e.g. to model weights, cloud access, or models themselves.
