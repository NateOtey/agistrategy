**What it is:** A Washington nonprofit that recruits people with technical backgrounds and places them, through paid fellowships, in congressional offices, executive agencies and think tanks to work on AI, biosecurity and other emerging-technology policy. By [early 2026](https://www.longtermwiki.com/resources/kb-9153b3b9aadf4568) it had helped more than 150 people into such roles, including in the White House, the Departments of Commerce, Energy, State and Homeland Security, and think tanks from CSIS to the Heritage Foundation.

**What it wants:** A government with enough technical expertise to understand and manage emerging technologies, including advanced AI.

**Resources and levers:** People in the rooms where policy is written. Fellows work as staff, not lobbyists, and often stay in government afterward. Partnerships across the political spectrum, including a 2025 AI leadership program run with the right-leaning Foundation for American Innovation and the Center for a New American Security. Funding from Coefficient Giving (formerly Open Philanthropy) and others.

**Obstacles and opponents:** Critics who see it as a channel for one funder's views into government; a 2023 *Politico* [investigation](https://news.yahoo.com/billionaire-backed-network-ai-advisers-090000786.html) described a "billionaire-backed network of AI advisers" in Washington, with Horizon at its center. Rules on outside-funded fellows in congressional offices. A political climate in which ties to the AI safety movement can be a liability.
