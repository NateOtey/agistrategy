**What it is:** Alphabet's AI lab, formed in 2023 by merging DeepMind (founded 2010 by Demis Hassabis, Shane Legg and Mustafa Suleyman) with Google Brain. It makes the Gemini models and AlphaFold, which won Hassabis a share of the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. In August 2026 [Hassabis stepped back](https://fortune.com/2026/08/05/demis-hassabis-steps-down-google-deepmind-ai-shakeup/) from day-to-day leadership to become its chairman and Alphabet's chief scientist, with Koray Kavukcuoglu taking over operations.

**What it wants:** To build AGI and use it to accelerate science, while keeping Google at the center of how people find information and get work done. Hassabis has long favored international cooperation on AI safety, comparing the ideal to a CERN for AGI.

**Resources and levers:** Arguably the strongest combination of assets in the industry: its own TPU chips, Google's data centers and cash flow, billions of users across Search, Android, Gmail and YouTube, and one of the deepest benches of research talent. A Frontier Safety Framework and dedicated safety teams. Google signed the White House Accord on Super Intelligence in 2026.

**Obstacles and opponents:** OpenAI and Anthropic at the frontier, and Meta and Chinese labs in open models. Pressure to ship fast while protecting a Search business that AI may cannibalize. The scale of the bet: Alphabet raised its 2026 capital spending guidance in July to [$195 to $205 billion](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/artificial-intelligence/alphabets-205-billion-spending-target-fuels-ai-cost-fears-2), more than double 2025, which investors will tolerate only as long as AI revenue keeps up. Antitrust cases against Google in the US and EU. Talent leaving for rivals and startups, and the departure of Chief Scientist Jeff Dean in the same reshuffle, to co-found a startup.
