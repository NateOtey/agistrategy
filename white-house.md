**What it is:** The Trump administration, which reversed the Biden administration's AI executive order on its first day in January 2025 and set out a pro-growth approach in its July 2025 [AI Action Plan](https://www.ai.gov/action-plan).

**What it wants:** American "dominance" in AI over China, through faster data-center construction, cheaper energy, fewer regulations and exports of US technology to allies. It wants a single federal rulebook that preempts state AI laws, models free of what it calls "woke" bias, and AI companies that cooperate with the government. Its March 2026 [National AI Legislative Framework](https://techpolicy.press/trump-and-gop-lawmakers-push-for-new-national-ai-legislation) also promises protections for children and against higher energy bills. Since September 2026 it has favored industry self-policing: on September 29 the President and the heads of Google, Anthropic, Meta, OpenAI, xAI and Nvidia signed the [White House Accord on Super Intelligence](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/trump-ai-giants-super-intelligence/), a voluntary pledge to adopt internal controls, independent audits and board oversight for frontier models. The same day, Trump ordered federal agencies to say "super intelligence" instead of "artificial intelligence."

**Resources and levers:** Executive orders, including one in December 2025 directing the Justice Department to challenge state AI laws. A new [Super Intelligence Force](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/trump-names-national-intelligence-director-jay-clayton-to-lead-a-new-federal-ai-task-force), announced October 4, 2026 and led by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton, to coordinate federal AI policy, identify security threats and, in the administration's words, resist "overregulation"; the Council on Foreign Relations has [suggested ways](https://www.cfr.org/articles/five-ways-to-make-the-new-ai-task-force-a-success) to make it work. Control of export licenses, which it has used to cut deals, like allowing Nvidia chip sales to China in return for a share of revenue. Federal procurement, which it used against Anthropic. The bully pulpit, and the ability to make or break a company's relationship with Washington.

**Obstacles and opponents:** States and state attorneys general, including Republicans, who resist preemption; the Senate voted 99 to 1 to drop a ten-year state-law moratorium in 2025. Courts: a district judge ruled the measures against Anthropic illegal in August 2026, though an appeals court let the Pentagon's designation stand. Safety advocates who see a voluntary accord as no substitute for law. The populist right, which fears job losses and Big Tech power. China. Rising electricity prices that voters blame on data centers.
