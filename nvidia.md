**What it is:** The company whose GPUs and software power most AI training worldwide, led by co-founder Jensen Huang since 1993. It became the world's most valuable company during the boom.

**What it wants:** To sell as many chips to as many customers as possible, including in China, and to keep its CUDA software as the industry standard. Huang argues that export controls backfire by pushing China to build its own chips, and that the US wins by having the world build on American technology.

**Resources and levers:** A dominant market share, deep margins, and the industry's most coveted product. Investments that tie customers to it, including in OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI and Mistral. Direct access to Trump, which helped win permission to sell its H20 chips in China in exchange for a 15% revenue share (2025) and then H200 chips with a 25% cut. Huang signed the White House Accord on Super Intelligence in 2026.

**Obstacles and opponents:** China hawks in Congress and the safety community who favor tight export controls; Anthropic and Dario Amodei have been especially vocal. Beijing, which has at times told Chinese firms to avoid its chips; months after Washington approved H200 sales, a Commerce official [testified](https://news.bgov.com/capital-markets/nvidia-has-sold-zero-h200s-to-china-top-us-export-enforcer-says) that Nvidia had sold none. Huawei. Custom chips from Google, Amazon and others. Circular financing: part of its sales depend on customers funded by Nvidia's own investments. Chip smugglers, whose activity draws calls for tracking features Nvidia opposes.
