**Definition:** Three escalating ways of controlling the speed of frontier AI development.

- **Pacing:** deliberately managing how fast the frontier advances, especially automated AI research, so that safety research, oversight, and institutions can keep up.
- **Pause:** a temporary stop on training or deploying more capable systems, usually until specified safety conditions are met.
- **Halt:** the ability, backed by agreement and technical means, to stop dangerous AI activities altogether if necessary.

**Example or analogy:** Think of road safety. Pacing is a speed limit, a pause is a red light, and a halt is closing the road; each is only as good as the ability to enforce it. In March 2023, an open letter organized by the Future of Life Institute called for a six-month pause on training systems more powerful than GPT-4. It gathered tens of thousands of signatures and no pause. (Widely signed, widely ignored: a combination familiar to anyone who has organized a petition.) In July 2026, a statement signed by more than 1,300 employees of frontier AI companies asked the US government to support an international effort to "deliberately pace the frontier of automated AI development," on the grounds that no company or country can safely slow down alone.

**Relevance to strategy:** Pacing is now one of the central strategic debates. The AI Futures Project's "Plan A" proposes a temporary, internationally verified slowdown while better verification and safety tools are built. Critics raise practical problems. Tom Davidson argues that pausing algorithmic progress while computing power keeps accumulating creates "dry tinder" for a dangerously fast breakout if the agreement collapses; others question whether any pause can be verified or enforced. MIRI researchers have studied the technical requirements for being able to halt dangerous AI activities at all, a capacity that does not yet exist.

**Where people disagree:** Whether slowing down is desirable, given the benefits of AI and the risk that rivals do not slow down too; whether it is feasible without strong verification; and whether a gradual pace or a hard stop is the safer target.
