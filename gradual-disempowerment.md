**Definition:** Gradual disempowerment is the slow loss of human influence over the economy, culture, and government as AI systems take over more of the work and decision-making that people used to do, without any single dramatic event or villain. The intelligence curse names one mechanism for it: when states and companies no longer depend on human labor, they lose much of their incentive to invest in people or answer to them.

**Example or analogy:** Economists have long observed a "resource curse": countries rich in oil often end up with weaker democracies and less investment in citizens, because the government's revenue comes from the ground instead of from taxing a productive population. Luke Drago and Rudolf Laine argue that advanced AI could produce the same pattern everywhere. If AI does most of the valuable work, ordinary people lose the bargaining power that has historically come from being needed, as workers, taxpayers, and soldiers.

**Relevance to strategy:** This risk could materialize even if every AI system is aligned and obedient, and even if no one acts in bad faith; each individual decision to hand a task to AI may be perfectly sensible. That puts it largely outside the reach of technical alignment work and squarely in the domain of economics, political institutions, and law. Proposed responses include policies that keep humans economically and politically relevant, broad ownership of AI-generated wealth, AI tools that strengthen human decision-making instead of replacing it, and institutions that measure and track human influence over key systems.

**Where people disagree:** Whether markets and democracies will adapt, as they did to earlier waves of automation, or whether AI that can do nearly all work breaks the historical pattern. Some also question whether gradual disempowerment is distinct from power concentration, since a disempowered majority usually implies an empowered few.
