**Who they are:** Paid, mentored programs, usually two to six months long, that take people who are already serious about AI safety and give them a project, a mentor and a network. The largest is [MATS](https://www.matsprogram.org/program/summer-2026), whose summer 2026 cohort in Berkeley and London had 120 fellows and 100 mentors, in tracks from empirical research to policy, security and field-building. Constellation's [Astra Fellowship](https://www.constellation.org/programs/astra-fellowship) runs for five months in Berkeley. The labs run their own: the [Anthropic Fellows Program](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/anthropic-fellows-program-2026/) has taken more than 200 people since early 2025. Policy-focused programs include the [GovAI](https://www.governance.ai/post/summer-fellowship-2026-research-track) and IAPS fellowships and [ERA](https://erafellowship.org/fellowship-1) in Cambridge, and shorter bootcamps such as ARENA teach the technical skills these programs expect.

**What they want:** To turn promising newcomers into productive researchers quickly, and to give the organizations that hire from them a low-risk way to try people out.

**Resources and levers:** Access to mentors from Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Redwood Research, METR, the UK AI Security Institute and other organizations in this deck. Stipends and compute: Astra pays $8,400 a month, and empirical fellows at Astra and Anthropic get roughly $15,000 a month of compute. Placement: more than 80% of Astra's first cohort and more than a third of past Anthropic fellows went on to full-time safety work, so these programs are, in practice, the field's main hiring funnel.

**Obstacles and opponents:** Mentor time. Acceptance rates are often [under 5%](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/aEiZfHAXeKXF5PJgH/ai-safety-has-a-scaling-problem), and the number of experienced people willing to supervise, not money, limits how many can be trained. A tilt toward people who can move to Berkeley or London for months at a time. The worry that lab-run and lab-adjacent programs mainly feed the frontier companies. Dependence on a small set of funders, led by Coefficient Giving.
