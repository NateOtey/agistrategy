**Who he is:** Anthropic's co-founder and CEO, a former physicist and OpenAI research VP who helped develop the scaling-law research behind today's models.

**What he wants:** He has written that powerful AI could compress decades of scientific progress into a few years (["Machines of Loving Grace,"](https://darioamodei.com/machines-of-loving-grace) 2024), and that getting there safely requires transparency rules for frontier labs, tight export controls on chips to China, and preparation for large job losses. In ["We Must Pace the Frontier"](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier) (September 2026) he went further: "We must slow the pace at which we improve the capabilities of AI models." His plan has three steps: outside evaluators embedded inside the labs; coordination among democratic countries, including capability "checkpoints" and possible limits on training compute; and, eventually, agreements with China ranging from bans on bioweapon uses to a speed limit on AI improving itself. Sam Altman and Elon Musk both [publicly agreed](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/amodei-calls-to-pace-the-frontier-altman-and-musk-agree/).

**Resources and levers:** Control of Anthropic and its policy voice. Long essays that set the terms of debate in Washington and Silicon Valley. Credibility with the safety community that few lab CEOs share, and a willingness to take public positions against his own government, as in the Pentagon dispute ("We're not going to move on those red lines"). He also signed the White House Accord on Super Intelligence in September 2026.

**Obstacles and opponents:** David Sacks and other administration allies, who have accused him of fearmongering for commercial advantage. Jensen Huang, over export controls. Pete Hegseth and the Department of War. Critics who argue that his warnings sit awkwardly beside his company's race to build the systems he warns about, and that pacing proposals tend to favor whoever is already in the lead.
