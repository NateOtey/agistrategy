**What it is:** The Chinese Communist Party and state under Xi Jinping, including the Cyberspace Administration of China, which licenses AI models, and the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, which steers industrial policy.

**What it wants:** Technological self-reliance, so that US export controls cannot cap China's progress; AI spread through the whole economy under its "AI+" initiative (2025); party control over what models say; and a larger voice in global AI rules. Xi has [proposed](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/china-s-xi-pushes-for-global-ai-body-at-apec-in-counter-to-us-ce7d5cd8dd8bf722) a World AI Cooperation Organization, pitched as an inclusive alternative to US-led arrangements.

**Resources and levers:** State direction of capital, land and power, including power grids that can bring data centers online faster than the US can. A large pool of AI researchers. Dominance in rare earths and other inputs, which it restricted in 2025 as leverage in trade talks. Control over which foreign chips its companies may buy: Beijing discouraged purchases of Nvidia's China-specific chips before [approving limited H200 imports](https://www.businessday.co.za/world/international-companies/2026-03-18-nvidia-cracks-beijings-nod-for-h200-chip-sales/) in 2026. A diplomatic pitch to the Global South built on open-weight models and cooperation.

**Obstacles and opponents:** US export controls on chips and chipmaking tools. Its own gap in advanced chip manufacturing. US allies, including the Netherlands, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan, that control key parts of the supply chain. Tension between censorship and capability.
