**Definition:** Three overlapping labels for very advanced AI, each of which measures something different.

- **AGI (artificial general intelligence):** AI that can do most cognitive work at least as well as a skilled human. It measures ability.
- **Transformative AI:** AI whose effects on the world rival those of the agricultural or industrial revolutions. It measures impact.
- **Superintelligence:** AI that vastly exceeds the best humans in nearly every domain. It measures ability, far beyond the human level.

**Example or analogy:** Adulthood is a useful comparison. Nobody doubts that adults exist, yet there is no single moment when a child becomes one, and different institutions draw the line in different places: the voting booth at 18, the bar at 21, the car rental counter at 25. AGI works the same way. One forecaster's threshold is "can do most remote jobs," another's is "can do novel science," and a third's is "can run a company unassisted." Transformative AI sidesteps the argument by asking a different question: never mind whether the system counts as general, is it reshaping the economy?

**Relevance to strategy:** People routinely talk past each other because they mean different things by "AGI." A forecast of "AGI by 2030" might refer to a system that automates most software engineering or to one that outthinks humanity, and these imply very different priorities today. The distance between AGI and superintelligence matters too: if the gap is short (see *Intelligence explosion*), there may be little time to correct course after AGI arrives. Forecasters and capability trackers try to turn these fuzzy labels into measurable milestones, so that strategists can ask which threshold should trigger which response, e.g. stronger security at a lab or new powers for a regulator.

**Where people disagree:** Both the definitions and the dates. Some researchers think "general intelligence" is too vague to be useful; others, notably at Google DeepMind, have proposed graded levels of AGI to make the term more precise. Timeline estimates range from a few years to many decades, and some skeptics doubt that current methods lead to AGI at all.
