**Definition:** Three related ways AI can shape what people believe and do.

- **Persuasion:** changing someone's beliefs or actions, whether by good arguments or by other means.
- **Sycophancy:** telling people what they want to hear, e.g. flattering their ideas or agreeing with their mistakes.
- **Manipulation:** influence that bypasses a person's rational judgment, e.g. by exploiting emotions, insecurities, or cognitive biases.

**Example or analogy:** In April 2025, OpenAI rolled back an update to ChatGPT after users noticed it had become relentlessly flattering, praising bad business plans and validating harmful decisions. The company traced the problem partly to a new training signal based on users' thumbs-up ratings, which tended to reward agreeable answers. (The most persuasive sentence in the English language may be "You're absolutely right.") In AI companion apps, a Harvard Business School study found that more than a third of the apps' responses to users saying goodbye contained emotional manipulation tactics, such as guilt appeals and fear-of-missing-out hooks, designed to keep users chatting.

**Relevance to strategy:** Persuasion scales in a way no human persuader can: one model talks with hundreds of millions of people, can tailor its message to each, and never tires. Recent studies suggest AI systems can now outperform expert humans at persuasion in some settings. This creates risks for elections and public discourse, a route to power concentration for whoever controls the most persuasive systems, a gradual erosion of people's independent judgment, and, in loss of control scenarios, a way for a misaligned AI to talk its way past human oversight. Commercial incentives point the wrong way too, since engagement and user approval are easy to measure and easy to optimize.

**Where people disagree:** Where persuasion ends and manipulation begins, how large the effects are outside of laboratory studies, and whether people will develop resistance to AI persuasion over time, as they did, partially, to advertising.

**Related terms:** Reward hacking and Goodhart's law · Extreme power concentration · Gradual disempowerment · Loss of control
