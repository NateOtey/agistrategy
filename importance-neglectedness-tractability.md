**Definition:** A rough framework for deciding which problems to work on. Importance (or scale) is how much good solving the problem would do. Neglectedness is how few resources are already going into it. Tractability (or solvability) is how much progress additional effort would buy. A problem that scores well on all three is a promising place to add effort.

**Example or analogy:** Compare AI safety with AI capabilities. Capabilities work is enormously important commercially, but so many people and dollars already go into it that one more researcher adds little. Safety is far more neglected: by one estimate, roughly $1,700 is spent building AI for every $1 spent making it safe (see *Players and Incentives*). Within safety, the same logic applies at a finer grain: a crowded research agenda can be less valuable to join than an unglamorous one that nobody owns. A restaurant on a street with no restaurants is not automatically a good idea, though; sometimes a problem is neglected because it is intractable, or not really a problem.

**Relevance to strategy:** Many people in the AI safety community first chose AI as a cause using this framework, and it is still useful for choosing a niche within the field: which threat model, which intervention, which kind of organization. It helps explain why advice changes over time. As an area fills with talented people and money, its neglectedness falls, and the most valuable place to add one more person can move elsewhere.

**Where people disagree:** Whether the three factors can really be separated or estimated, given that many of the most important questions (how hard alignment is, how much time remains) are deeply uncertain. Critics also argue that the framework can make people chase whatever looks neglected rather than build depth, and that it underrates the value of working on popular problems where institutions and coalitions already exist.
