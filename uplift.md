**Definition:** How much AI improves what a person or organization could accomplish without it. The term shows up in two different conversations.

- **AI R&D uplift:** how much AI speeds up the work of AI researchers. This is a key input into forecasts of recursive self-improvement.
- **Misuse uplift:** how much AI helps a would-be attacker beyond what is already available from search engines, textbooks, and experts, especially for biological, chemical, or cyber attacks.

**Example or analogy:** Think of a calculator in a math class. Everyone agrees the calculator helps; the interesting question is how much it adds beyond what the student could already do with a pencil, and for which students. Uplift studies are built on the same logic: give one group access to an AI model and another group only the internet, then compare how far each gets. A 2024 RAND red-team study of that kind found no statistically significant difference in the quality of biological attack plans produced with and without the language models of the time.

**Relevance to strategy:** Uplift is the counterfactual question that matters. Whether a model can explain how something dangerous works is less important than whether it makes the dangerous thing achievable for someone who could not otherwise do it. Misuse uplift drives decisions about safeguards, access restrictions, and the capability thresholds in frontier safety frameworks. AI R&D uplift drives timelines and the case for pacing: if AI makes researchers twice as productive, and the next generation makes them four times as productive, the calendar starts to compress.

**Where people disagree:** How to measure uplift well, and whether results from earlier models still hold for newer ones. There is also debate about whose uplift matters most. A model that helps novices may expand the pool of possible attackers, while a model that helps experts may enable more sophisticated attacks.
