**What it is:** A philanthropic advisory firm, founded in 2018 by Natalie Cargill, that helps wealthy donors give to reduce catastrophic risks, above all from AI. Most of its money moves as recommendations to individual donors; it also runs its own pooled funds, including a Frontier AI Fund for donors giving $100,000 or more a year and an Emerging Challenges Fund open to anyone.

**What it wants:** To bring new money into AI safety and governance beyond the handful of funders who have dominated the field, and to steer it toward the most useful projects.

**Resources and levers:** Fast-growing money. By mid-2026 it reported [$266 million](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/GuwpKBEZwpw3ySmox/ai-safety-funder-bulletin) in total AI risk-reduction giving since 2018, up from $89 million at the end of 2025, putting it on track to be one of the largest AI safety funders after Coefficient Giving. Grantees have included METR and organizations that worked on the EU AI Act's code of practice. Access to new donors in finance and tech, cultivated through private dinners and retreats.

**Obstacles and opponents:** Dependence on a small number of very large donors, whose priorities can shift. Critics, including some within effective altruism, who question whether its grantmakers have the political expertise that policy funding now requires. The same "doomer funding" attacks from the tech right that target Coefficient Giving, which is also its largest institutional backer.
