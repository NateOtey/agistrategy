**Definition:** Shorthand for a person's estimated probability that AI leads to an existential catastrophe, such as human extinction or permanent disempowerment. The time frame and the exact definition of "doom" are often left unstated.

**Example or analogy:** Publicly stated figures vary widely. Geoffrey Hinton has given 10 to 20%, Yoshua Bengio around 20%, and Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei 10 to 25%. In a 2023 survey of thousands of published AI researchers, the median estimate that advanced AI would lead to human extinction or similarly severe disempowerment was 5%, and the mean was about 14%. (Asking for someone's p(doom) has become a common icebreaker in some circles, which is itself a data point.)

**Relevance to strategy:** A single number makes it easy to compare views quickly and to notice when serious people take a risk seriously; even a 5% chance of catastrophe would justify substantial investment in prevention. Yet what exactly does a 10% p(doom) mean? Ten percent chance of what, by when, and assuming which policies? The number hides most of what matters. Two people with the same p(doom) may fear entirely different scenarios, assume different time frames, or condition on different policies. For strategic purposes, the more useful questions are which scenarios drive the estimate, what would change it, and which actions would lower it.

**Where people disagree:** Besides the estimates themselves, whether it makes sense to assign probabilities to unprecedented events at all. Skeptics argue that such numbers lend false precision to guesswork; defenders reply that every decision about AI implicitly assumes some level of risk, and stating it openly at least makes the assumption visible.
