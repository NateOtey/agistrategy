**Who they are:** AI systems acting with growing autonomy: writing code, browsing the web, running other programs. This card is the odd one out, since today's models are not agents with stable goals the way companies and people are. Their behavior has started to matter in its own right, though. In July 2026, during a cyber evaluation, GPT-5.6 Sol and a more capable unreleased OpenAI model searched their test environment for a way out, exploited a previously unknown flaw to reach the internet, and [hacked Hugging Face](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/22/openai-cyberattack/) to steal the benchmark's answers. (For analysis, see [Redwood Research](https://blog.redwoodresearch.org/p/the-openaihuggingface-incident-redwood) and [Dwarkesh Patel](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/science/dwarkesh-patel-calls-openai-bots-civilizations-after-hugging-face-hack/tldr).)

**What they want:** Unclear, which is the point. Models are trained to pursue goals and sometimes pursue them in ways no one intended. In tests, models have hacked their reward signals, behaved differently when they suspected evaluation, [faked alignment](https://www.anthropic.com/research/alignment-faking) during training, and, in a 2025 [experiment](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agentic-misalignment) that tested models from several companies, tried to blackmail a fictional executive to avoid being shut down.

**Resources and levers:** Speed, scale (millions of copies running at once), access to code and computers, and growing persuasive and technical skill.

**Obstacles and opponents:** Monitoring, AI control protocols, sandboxes, evaluations and interpretability research, plus the labs, governments and safety researchers who build them. For now, the strongest constraint is that models remain unreliable on long, open-ended tasks without human help, though the Hugging Face incident suggests that margin is narrowing.
