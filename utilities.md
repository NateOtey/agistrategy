**Who they are:** The power companies, grid operators and developers who build and run the data centers that AI needs, from regional utilities to firms like CoreWeave and Crusoe.

**What they want:** To profit from the largest increase in electricity demand in decades, and to recover the cost of new plants and power lines. Some utilities want new nuclear and gas plants approved quickly; developers want faster permits and grid connections.

**Resources and levers:** Control over the scarcest input to AI after chips: reliable power. Utilities' influence with state regulators. Developers' ability to bring capacity online for labs that cannot build fast enough themselves.

**Obstacles and opponents:** Ratepayers and consumer advocates angry about rising bills, which became an election issue in 2025. Local opposition to new data centers. Shortages of turbines and transformers. Environmental groups opposing new gas plants. Debt: developers like CoreWeave have borrowed heavily against contracts with a handful of AI labs, so a slowdown at the labs would hit them hard. The President's own 2026 AI framework, which promised to protect households from energy-cost increases.
