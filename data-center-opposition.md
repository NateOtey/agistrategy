**What it is:** A loose movement of residents, local officials, farmers and environmental groups opposing new data centers near them. It spans party lines and has grown quickly; by mid-2026 a growing number of cities had [paused new data-center approvals](https://wisconsinexaminer.com/2026/05/28/repub/more-cities-are-pressing-pause-on-data-centers-as-local-backlash-grows/).

**What it wants:** Protection from higher electricity bills, water use, noise, lost farmland and diesel or gas generators, and a fairer share of the benefits when projects go ahead.

**Resources and levers:** Zoning boards, city councils, state utility commissions and lawsuits. Votes: energy prices helped flip several state utility-commission seats in 2025. Allies in national politics, including Bernie Sanders on the left and populists on the right.

**Obstacles and opponents:** Developers, utilities and the tech companies behind them, which bring promises of tax revenue and jobs. State governments competing to attract investment. Federal efforts to speed permitting for AI infrastructure.
