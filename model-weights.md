**Definition:** A model's weights are the billions of numbers, learned during training, that make up the trained model. Whoever holds the weights holds the model: they can run it, copy it, modify it, and strip out its safeguards. Model security is the work of keeping weights, and the systems used to train and run them, out of the wrong hands.

**Example or analogy:** Training a frontier model can cost hundreds of millions of dollars in computing power. Copying the resulting weights takes a single file transfer. Stealing a model's weights is therefore the equivalent of stealing the finished product and the factory at once, at the price of a large download. A 2024 RAND study concluded that securing weights requires many layered measures instead of a few silver bullets, and that defending against the most capable attackers, such as elite state-sponsored hacking teams, will require far more investment than most companies have made.

**Relevance to strategy:** Weight security underpins nearly every other safeguard. Safety training, usage monitoring, and deployment restrictions mean little once a model has been stolen, and export controls on chips mean little if a rival can simply take the finished product. Security also shapes racing dynamics, since a lead that can be stolen is no lead at all. Strategists therefore treat security as a precondition for many other plans, including any international agreement that depends on keeping dangerous capabilities contained. Threats come from outside hackers and also from insiders, which makes personnel security and internal access controls part of the picture.

**Where people disagree:** How much security is achievable at reasonable cost, how much governments should help or require it, and how weight security fits with the case for openly releasing some models.
