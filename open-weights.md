**Definition:** An open-weight model is one whose weights are published for anyone to download, run, and modify. A closed model is available only through the developer's own products or online interface, so the developer controls how it is used. Structured access describes the middle ground, in which developers grant different levels of access to different users, e.g. researchers or vetted businesses.

**Example or analogy:** Publishing open weights is like publishing a recipe; offering a closed model is like selling meals at a restaurant. Anyone can cook from a recipe, adapt it, and share their version, which spreads good cooking far and wide. The restaurant can refuse service, change the menu, or stop serving a dish that turns out to be dangerous. (A recipe, once published, cannot be unpublished.) As of October 2026, several leading Chinese developers and some US companies release open-weight models, while most of the most capable US models remain closed.

**Relevance to strategy:** The choice pulls different risks in opposite directions. Open weights support research, scrutiny, competition, and access for people outside wealthy companies and countries, which guards against power concentrating in a few hands. They also make safeguards easy to remove, make misuse impossible to monitor, and make a release impossible to recall. This is why many proposals treat openness as a question of capability level: release weaker models openly and restrict the most dangerous ones. The question also links to geopolitics, since open models shape which countries' technology the rest of the world builds on.

**Where people disagree:** Where the dangerous threshold lies, whether open models today provide much uplift beyond what is already available, and whether restricting open release would mainly hand advantage to closed developers and to rivals who will not restrict theirs.
