**Definition:** A theory of change is an explicit, step-by-step account of how a particular action leads to the outcome you want. A theory of victory, a term borrowed from military strategy, is the same thing at the largest scale: a description of what success in the AGI transition looks like for the world as a whole, and a plausible path to get there.

**Example or analogy:** A theory of change for an individual might run: "I will work on chip export policy, because it buys democracies time; that time lets safety research catch up; and safety research reduces the chance of loss of control." Each link in the chain can be examined and challenged. The AI Futures Project's "Plan A" is an example of a theory of victory: an international coalition audits the global supply of AI chips, temporarily slows frontier development while verification tools are built, and then resumes progress under transparency and safety-case regulation until the risks of superintelligence are judged very low. Readers may find each step more or less convincing, which is the point of writing it down.

**Relevance to strategy:** Anyone trying to help has to choose a focus and make a plan, and a theory of change is what turns a vague intention to help into a plan that can be tested. Writing one out exposes hidden assumptions, shows which links are weakest, and reveals when an action only makes sense under one particular set of beliefs about timelines, threat models, or politics. Comparing theories of victory, as the AI 2040 project does across several possible plans, also clarifies how different efforts fit together, and where they conflict.

**Where people disagree:** Which theories of victory are realistic. Some favor international coordination and pacing; others favor a democratic lead in AI, defensive technology, or betting on alignment research to succeed in time. There is also a methodological debate between those who think detailed plans are essential and those who think the future is too uncertain for detailed planning, and that it is better to build broadly useful capacity and adapt as events unfold.
