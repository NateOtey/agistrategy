**Definition:** Informal labels for groups with different views on AI. They are widely used, often loosely, and sometimes as insults.

- **Doomer:** someone who thinks advanced AI has a high chance of causing human extinction or permanent catastrophe. The label is usually applied by critics; many people with these concerns reject it and describe themselves as worried about existential risk.
- **Accelerationist, or e/acc (effective accelerationism):** someone who believes faster AI progress is overwhelmingly good and that most safety concerns are overblown, or serve as cover for incumbents who want regulation that protects them. The e/acc movement emerged online in 2022 and drew wide attention in 2023.
- **AI ethics vs. AI safety:** a long-running split between two research communities. AI ethics has focused on present-day harms such as bias, surveillance, labor exploitation, misinformation, and the concentration of corporate power. AI safety has focused more on risks from future, more capable systems, including loss of control. The two have often clashed over priorities and funding, though many people now work across both.

**Example or analogy:** Camp labels work much like political labels: useful as shorthand, and a poor substitute for knowing what someone actually thinks. Someone can expect AGI soon and be optimistic about it, or expect it decades away and still be worried. (Labels, like nicknames, tend to be chosen by other people.)

**Relevance to strategy:** These labels shape who talks to whom, which coalitions form, and how policy proposals are received. A proposal associated with one camp can be dismissed by another before anyone reads it. Coalitions across camps, e.g. safety and ethics advocates who agree on transparency or liability rules, have sometimes accomplished more than either camp alone. Being able to state each camp's strongest case is a valuable skill in policy and coalition-building, as is noticing when a label is being used to dismiss an argument instead of answering it.

**Where people disagree:** Nearly everything, which is the point. The most useful habit is to translate a label into specific positions on the questions that drive disagreement: how soon advanced AI will arrive, how hard alignment is, and how far to trust governments versus companies. Most of the actual disagreement lives there.
